# Reflect core

The headless core of Reflect, in plain TypeScript with no database and no Next.js. The web app imports it as `@reflect/core/<module>`. The plan for it is `docs/design/HEADLESS_REFLECT_2026-11-10.md`: one core, with a git repository or our Postgres as the host of a person's records.

## Where things are

| Path | Holds |
|---|---|
| `src/structure.ts`, `src/onboarding.json` | The onboarding questions and the stances a hold can take, as typed values. |
| `src/person.ts`, `src/onboarding.ts` | The person's stores as pure functions over `PersonState`, and the onboarding reducer Me still uses. |
| `src/journal.ts`, `src/packs.ts`, `src/reflect.ts` | Entries and prompts, prompt packs, and reflecting with frames. |
| `src/store.ts` | The store interface, `Journal`: one person's journal, append-only, whichever host keeps it. |
| `src/layout.ts` | Where records sit in an instance folder: a vault (the lifetime folders, `journal/`, `reflections/`, `concepts/`, `documents/`, `telos/`, `intents/` at the root; `work/` ventures left to Naive) or the older `people/<id>/` layout. |
| `src/lifetime.ts` | The Ventures of a Lifetime as folders: each folder and its guide, the legacy templates, the eight needs and how each is met (`keepNeed`), the regions and their states with dated passages and the state rebuilt from them (`keepPassage`, `lifeState`), the legacy prompts, and their checks. Folders are regions and associations, never states. |
| `src/files-journal.ts` | The folder host: entries as `journal/<date>-<id>.md` (front matter optional, so a hand-written file is an entry; front matter the person adds is kept on later writes), reflections as `reflections/<entry>.json` (labelings an older version wrote are kept as they are and never read), sparks with their concept notes in `concepts/`. |
| `src/learning.ts` | What the person knows, in `learn/`, with books at the center: books (`learn/books/`, status and dates, the subjects they cover), subjects (`learn/subjects/`, nodes of the graph with `requires`, `part_of` and `same_as`, aspire or need, and dated grasp lines the person gives: teach, explain, gist or forgot), resources (`learn/resources/`, other links, including posts and threads whose `source` (x, substack, reddit, youtube) is read from the link), films (`learn/films/`), talks (`learn/talks/`, someone the person saw speak) and conversations (`learn/conversations/`). Subjects are inputs, fields the person studies; `shown by` lines (someone saw the person display knowledge) and `suggested by` lines are never grasps. A book or film can be `referenced` (cited, how far read not said). `learnMap` derives known from the latest grasp, shown and suggested apart from it, the subjects of a read book not yet rated, and the fringe. Nothing is known until the person rates it. `learnProblems` checks it. |
| `src/ideas.ts` | The person's own ideas, outputs kept apart from learn (`ideas/`): each restatement is a dated `stated` line, inputs are `from` (the person says it fed the idea) or `maybe from` (they suspect so), and recommendations are a separate list placed `supports`, `extends`, `parallels`, `challenges` or `opposes`, with who suggested them. A target is a vault note or the person's words with an optional link. Lines are never rewritten; `foldIdea` lets the latest line about a thing decide where it stands, and the web app folds its rows with the same code. `ideaProblems` checks it. `ideasNaming` lists the ideas that name one thing (a book by its ref), each with how: came from it, may have, or its place on the spectrum. |
| `src/naive-handoff.ts` | Sending an idea to Naive on the person's say-so. Reflect never imports Naive: `sendIdea` runs the person's own `naive capture` (or `NAIVE_BIN`) in their Naive workspace (`findNaiveWorkspace` looks above the vault for `naive-workspace.json`), with `handoffText`: the title, the latest statement, the `from` inputs and a line naming the idea note. Maybe-inputs and recommendations stay in Reflect. The idea then gets a dated `sent to naive <workspace>:raw/<file>.md` line (`SENT_LINE`, read by `sentOf`). |
| `src/catalog.ts` | `index.md`, the vault's catalog, where the person's own AI starts reading. `buildIndex` lists every note by folder with one wikilinked line (ideas with their statement and inputs, subjects by grasp or want, books, films, talks, conversations, links, concepts, ventures under `work/`, sources) and the folder guides; journal entries are listed by date only, never their text. `writeIndex` rewrites it and reports whether anything changed. It is derived from the notes and holds no fact of its own. |
| `src/wiki.ts` | The wiki: a compiled view of the vault written by the person's own AI, kept apart from the record (after Farza's personal wiki skill). `wikiOn` turns it on (off by default). `wikiPrompt` gives the AI the writer's instructions, `index.md`, the wiki so far and up to 15 journal entries not yet absorbed (pointer entries read live). `readWikiReply` checks each article (`checkArticle`): a `<folder>/<slug>` path, no em dash, every paragraph, list item and table row citing a note in the record (a "See also" line of wiki links is exempt), and every quote of four words or more found in what its passage cites; refused articles are listed with why, and the rest wait as a draft in `.reflect/`. `keepWikiDraft` writes the chosen articles (front matter with type, aliases, related, cited sources), logs absorbed entries in `wiki/_absorb.json` and rebuilds `wiki/_index.md` and `wiki/_backlinks.json`. A pattern, tension, identity, philosophy, life theme, assessment or metaphor is `reading: ai` until `confirmReading`; the confirmation holds for that text's hash, so a rewrite makes it a reading again. `wikiProblems` names an article citing a note that left the vault. |
| `src/sources.ts` | The person's other folders, pointed at instead of moved in. `addSource` names a folder outside the vault in `sources.json`; `indexSource` fingerprints its files into `.reflect/index/` (derived, git-ignored), leaving out hidden folders, dependencies, exclusions and names that look like secrets. `placementPrompt` asks the person's own AI where each open file belongs (book, film, talk, link, conversation, subject, idea, entry, venture, document or none); `readPlacements` reads its reply, keeping only files in the index; `keepPlacements` turns each chosen one into a dated `pointer <source>:<path> #<sha>` line on its note and a line in `sources/<name>.md`. An entry (a plain text file) becomes a closed journal entry whose front matter is `pointer: <source>:<path>` and `pointer_sha`; `filesJournal` reads its text from the file each time (`pointerText`), so it is listed and reflected on without a copy, and placing it again keeps the same entry. A venture maps to its Naive folder `work/<slug>` when there is one (Reflect never writes inside it); without one, a venture with a statement is kept as an idea with the pointer, and one with neither is only mapped, with a note saying so. A document becomes a custody card whose `where` is the file. Nothing in a source is moved, edited or copied. `sourceStatus` and `sourceProblems` report placed files that changed or vanished. |
| `src/sweep.ts` | The memory sweep: `SWEEP_PROMPT` for the person's own AI, `readSweep` for its JSON reply (`reflect.learn-source` v1), and `keepSweep` for the items the person chose. Inputs and ideas are kept apart; an idea's input the AI only inferred is kept as maybe from; nothing sets a grasp. |
| `src/openlibrary.ts` | Looking a book up in Open Library: `searchBooks` by title or author, `browseClass` for one Library of Congress main class (best rated first, paged), `lookupIsbn` for one edition with its work's subjects and table of contents, all over an injected fetch. `cleanEntry` cuts an entry that came back from a browser to the fields and sizes Reflect keeps. The catalog is editable by anyone, so its entry is a suggestion saved beside the book note (`learn/books/<book>.openlibrary.json`); the book note changes only by the person. Each entry also keeps the work's Library of Congress call numbers (`lcc`), which put the book on its shelf, and its cover id (`cover`, drawn by `coverUrl`). `trendingBooks` is what is read on Open Library this week; `workEntry` looks one work up by id to fill in call numbers. `browseQuery` runs any search query (as `themaQuery` writes them), best rated or most logged first; entries in lists carry readers' ratings (`rating`). `workDetails` reads what a book page shows: the work's description, its rating and its editions (English first). Lists are in one language, `LANGUAGE` (English for now): searches keep works with an English edition (`language:eng`) and show that edition's title and cover, this week's reads keep the ones with an English edition, and an ISBN search finds any language. Authors: entries keep their authors' ids (`authorKeys`); `authorDetails` reads one author (life dates, biography, photo for `authorPhotoUrl`, Wikipedia link), `authorWorks` lists their books, and `searchAuthors` finds authors by name, leaving out one-work duplicates. Lists can be in four orders, `SORTS`: best rated, most read, newest, oldest. `workAccess` reads where a work is free from the catalog's own links (the Internet Archive's scan, public or to borrow; Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks and LibriVox ids); `booksLike` lists the most read books on a work's first subject that is not too broad (`BROAD_SUBJECT`); `workDetails` also keeps editions' covers and page counts and the first English table of contents. |
| `src/claims.ts` | Claims in Learn: something a person holds true or false, with holders named and cited. The stance is one of the three doxastic attitudes (belief, disbelief, suspension of judgment); stances are dated lines, never changed, and `currentStance` takes the latest. `cleanClaim` checks a claim and its holders. |
| `src/thema.ts` | Learn's categories: Thema, the book trade's subject scheme (EDItEUR v1.7, free to use), its 20 sections as parents and two-letter categories as children, with Thema's own headings. Open Library does not carry Thema, so `categoryOf` places a book: fiction and children's books by their catalog subjects (genre), then its call numbers through Reflect's own crosswalk to Library of Congress ranges (narrowest range wins), then subjects; when nothing places it, nothing is guessed. `themaQuery` writes the Open Library search for a section or category (call-number ranges where it has them, subjects otherwise, fiction without children's books). `shelveThema` puts books in sections and subjects where most of their books are. `showCall` prints a call number as a library does. |
| `src/gutenberg.ts` | Project Gutenberg's plain texts (no key) for the reader inside Reflect: `gutenbergBook` fetches one by id, `chaptersOf` cuts the header and licence, joins hard-wrapped lines into paragraphs and splits the text into chapters (headings in title case; a text with none is cut into parts). Nothing is saved. `findGutenberg` finds a book's number in Gutenberg's own catalog search by title and author (`isTheBook`), since Open Library links only some. `rdfEnglish` reads the English title and author Gutenberg gives a text in another language. |
| `src/standardebooks.ts` | Standard Ebooks' proofread, typeset public-domain books (no key): `standardBook` reads one book's single-page edition, and `standardChapters` cuts its body matter into chapters of plain paragraphs, taking note marks out of the text and keeping where they sat; `standardNotes` reads the endnotes. Nothing is saved. |
| `src/reading.ts` | Reading arithmetic: words and minutes per chapter (238 words a minute), time left in the chapter and the book, `searchBook` and `findSpans` for a phrase in the text, and `carryPlace` to carry a kept place to a text with another chapter count. |
| `src/wiktionary.ts` | A word looked up while reading: `definitionsOf` reads the English senses from Wiktionary wikitext with their usage labels, marking older meanings (archaic, obsolete, dated); `lookupWord` takes one word from a selection. |
| `src/free.ts` | Whether a book can be read or heard in Reflect: `freeIndex` indexes a catalog of free books (lines of id, title and author) by title word, and `findFree` finds a book in it by every word of its title and a name of its author; `freeEditions` and `editionsOfId` find every text of the same book, each translation with its translators. `openlibrary.ts` marks each entry with `free` once the app calls `knowFree`. |
| `src/excerpts.ts` | Highlights in a text read in Reflect: `locate` finds a kept highlight again (at its place while the words there are its quote, else by its words), `runsOf` cuts a paragraph into plain and highlighted runs, `cutRuns` cuts them again where note marks sit, `citeQuote` writes a quote with its author, book and place, `HIGHLIGHT_COLORS` are the colours a highlight can have, and `excerptsMarkdown` writes excerpts as Markdown by book. |
| `src/listening.ts` | Reading and listening together: `matchSections` pairs a text's chapters with an audiobook's recorded parts by what each is called ("Chapter V" and "Chapter 05" are both `sectionKey` "chapter 5"), else one for one when the counts agree. |
| `src/hardcover.ts` | Edition rankings from Hardcover's GraphQL API (`hardcoverBook` over an injected query): a work is found by its editions' ISBNs, else by title and author in Hardcover's search, and its editions are ranked by Hardcover readers, Reflect's language first, with average rating, translators, publisher, year, pages and format. Only aggregate numbers, as Hardcover's terms allow for a public site; `hardcoverAuth` takes a token pasted with or without "Bearer". |
| `src/habits.ts` | Reading habits: `streak` (days in a row to today), `calendar` (weeks of days read, Monday first) and `byMonth` (a year's finished books by month). |
| `src/kindle.ts` | `parseClippings` reads a Kindle's My Clippings.txt: each highlight with its book, author, page and location, the note typed on it, bookmarks dropped, the last version of a changed highlight kept. |
| `src/lists.ts` | Lists of books chosen by others, each with its source (the Harvard Classics, Eliot, 1909-1910): `LISTS`, `listById`, `listBookQuery`, the Open Library search that finds one book of a list, `bestListMatch`, which picks the book itself over a collection that holds it, and `pathProgress`, how many of a list read in order are read and which is next. The Last Days of Socrates is read in the order of the events. |
| `src/librivox.ts` | LibriVox's free public-domain audiobooks (no key): `librivoxBook` reads the first English recording among a work's LibriVox ids, with its chapters, over https from the Internet Archive only. Nothing is saved. |
| `src/googlebooks.ts` | Google Books for reading on a book page: the whole book when it is free, else a preview, from the English edition of the same book with the most to read, shown in Google's reader (which searches inside the book). Also its description and link. Google's ratings are not used. Needs `GOOGLE_BOOKS_API_KEY`; without it, nothing is asked. Never saved. |
| `src/ways.ts` | Learn's ways of learning, after Aristotle: teaching (Posterior Analytics I.1, Metaphysics I.1), dialectic (Topics I.1-2), imitation (Poetics 4 and 9) and practice (Nicomachean Ethics II.1), with Rest and leisure apart (Nicomachean Ethics X.6, Politics VIII.5). `MEDIA` gives each medium its way and its status words; music, art and games count as learning only with a lesson. `bookWay` gives a book's way from its Thema category (stories under imitation), and `placeOf` says where an item is kept; a book on dialogues goes under dialectic. Articles are a medium of teaching. |
| `src/lookup.ts` | Filling in an item: `linkKind` and `lookLink` read a pasted link's public data (YouTube and Vimeo oEmbed, Spotify, SoundCloud, Apple Podcasts, Steam, the Internet Archive, the Art Institute of Chicago), and `searchMedium` searches the free catalog for a medium (Wikidata, the Internet Archive's public-domain films, MusicBrainz, Apple's podcast search, TVmaze, Steam, the Art Institute of Chicago). Fetching is passed in, so it runs anywhere. |
| `src/courses.ts` | Courses taken in Reflect: `courseSite` knows Open Yale and MIT OpenCourseWare course pages, `courseLectures` reads a course's lectures and `courseLecture` one lecture's video, audio, overview and transcript (Yale's chapters with times, MIT's captions as half-minute passages); `passageAt` finds the passage being spoken. Nothing is copied. |
| `src/podcasts.ts` | Podcasts heard in Reflect: `feedOf` finds a show's feed from its Apple Podcasts link, `readFeed` reads its episodes (audio, date, length, the Podcasting 2.0 transcript tag) with a stable `episodeKey`, and `episodeTranscript` reads a published transcript. |
| `src/browse-media.ts` | Browse beyond books: `BROWSE_MEDIA` in menu order, `browseWay`, `playsHere` (whether Reflect can play or show a find), Open Yale's catalog (`oycCatalog`), MIT's courses with lecture videos (`mitCourses`, MIT Learn), the Art Institute's public-domain works (`findArt`), the Internet Archive's public-domain films (`freeFilmsToWatch`) and `findMedium`. |
| `src/item-imports.ts` | Reading other apps' exports as items: Letterboxd CSV, podcast OPML, YouTube Takeout watch history, Spotify streaming history (tallied, with minutes) and a plain list; `readImport` caps at 500 and `notKept` drops titles already kept. |
| `src/item-lists.ts` | Lists beyond books, each credited to its source (Open Yale Courses, MIT OpenCourseWare, Harvard's Justice, famous debates, the Greek tragedies, the Vatican's 1995 films), with links only where the publisher's page was checked; `starters` ranks their items by a person's subjects for each way. |
| `src/media.ts` | Players and transcripts: `embedOf` gives the player for a link (YouTube with a start, Vimeo, the Internet Archive, Spotify, SoundCloud), `seconds` and `clock` read and write times, `readTranscript` reads a pasted transcript (YouTube's copy, times inline, VTT or SRT) into timed lines and `searchTranscript` finds words in it. |
| `src/shelves.ts` | The 21 main classes of the Library of Congress Classification, kept for reading call numbers; Learn's shelves are Thema sections now (`thema.ts`). `shelve` puts a book on the class most of its call numbers point to and a subject on the class most of its books are on; a book with no call number or a subject with no shelved book stays unshelved, and only filled shelves are kept unless `all` is asked for. Subjects found through Wikidata's LCC property (P1149) are not built. |
| `src/transcript.ts` | A transcript as courses: `draftTranscript` asks a model for rows and `anchorCourses` keeps only rows whose title (and grade) are in the text; `keepCourse` writes a confirmed row to `learn/courses/` linked to a subject. The grade is the school's; grasp is the person's. |
| `src/credentials.ts` | Credentials (`learn/credentials/`), with `checkBadge` reading an Open Badges assertion; vouches as dated lines on a subject (`keepVouches`); recommendations kept whole (`learn/vouches/`). None set grasp. |
| `src/linkedin.ts` | Reads a LinkedIn data export: endorsements, recommendations, certifications and listed skills, columns found by name (`parseCsv`, `linkedInDate`, `readLinkedInExport`). |
| `src/documents.ts` | Official documents as a custody checklist: the catalog of document types (grouped after FEMA's Emergency Financial First Aid Kit), how each is normally kept as of 2026-11 in the United States and what we recommend, custody cards (`documents/<type>.md`), connections (`connections/<id>.json`, a service and the types it holds, never credentials) and their checks. |
| `src/record-io.ts` | Writes one record and logs it in `memory.jsonl`. |
| `src/release.ts` | The release plan (`legacy/release.json`): trustees, confirmations, the wait, and gifts, with the hard rule that only `legacy/` and entries marked `legacy: true` can be given (`giftRefusal`), and `previewRelease`, what any release would hand each person. The Reflect app carries a release out (`../../web/src/server/legacy-store.ts`); a person's AI sends the plan with `keep_legacy_plan`. |
| `src/spark.ts` | Object writing: the forms (1, 5, 10, 15 minutes or one sentence), concrete nouns to start from, the prompt, and a spark's front matter and concept note. |
| `src/memory.ts` | Memory: `memory.jsonl`, one hash-chained line per write (`verifyMemory` finds a changed or removed line). |
| `src/journal-contract.ts` | The behaviour every host must have. The folder host runs it in `files-journal.test.ts`, and the Postgres host (`postgresJournal` in `../../web/src/server/journal-store.ts`) in its own tests against Neon. |
| `src/instance.ts` | An instance in a folder: `initInstance` (writes the vault and `template/reflect.yml` as the instance's workflow), `readConfig`, `checkInstance`, `adopt`. |
| `src/ask.ts` | The chat's instructions. `ASK_SYSTEM` tells the model to talk in plain text and to call the apps' tools to show records (shelves, ways, claims, a library search or class, a claim to propose, an item from the free catalogs, a transcript's words), never to answer from memory, invent books or citations, guess the person's stance, or say it saved anything. `cleanProposal` keeps a proposed claim to what Reflect can keep and never lists the person as its holder; `lccClass` takes only a real class letter. `learnSummary` is all the model sees of the person: book titles with status, items with medium and status, subject names, claims with stance. |
| `src/chat-journal.ts` | A chat kept for the chat history, stored with the journal's records: one entry per chat, marked by `CHAT_PROMPT`, one part per message written by `chatPart` as "You: ..." or "Reflect: ..." and read back by `readChatPart` (a part with no speaker is the person's own note). `isChatId` takes only a uuid, since the chat's id is the entry's id. |
| `src/log.ts` | The log: `learnActs` turns Learn's records into acts (putting a book or item on the shelves is an intention; starting, finishing, keeping a claim, changing a stance, rating a subject, a practice session and a lesson are acts), `actText` says one in plain words, and `logByDay` groups them by the person's day, newest first. |
| `src/items.ts` | Items and their pipeline (Observe, Reflect, Examine, Plan, Act): the event types, `refuse` (the item statechart and what each move needs, with the reason), `apply` and `fold` (an item is a fold over its events), `allowedMoves`, `phaseOf`, `eventText` and `readMove` (a move from a form, checked field by field). Reflect's outcomes held, revised, unsure, dropped; Examine's steps and its two outcome axes (what is believed, how it stands); Act's statuses scheduled, in progress, done, missed, where missed goes back to Observe only by observing why. No database and no network. |
| `src/quiz.ts` | Quizzing yourself on a lecture: `quizPrompt` asks a model for questions on what the lecture said and why from its numbered transcript, `parseQuiz` keeps the well-formed ones with the lecture's answer and passage, and `gaps` lists what the person did not know yet. |
| `src/learn-reason.ts` | Why a person is learning something, asked before a course: `reasonBody` writes what is causing them to learn it and for the sake of what under two headings, `reasonParts` reads them back, and `END_KINDS` are for its own sake or for a use. |
| `src/works.ts` | The journal as a library of works: `KINDS` (entry, essay, letter, plan, examination, exercise, decision, memoir, with the Library of Congress genre/form term where one fits), `TEMPLATES` (the prompt library: blank first, then templates for kinds of thinking, each with its source), `workTitle`, `cleanWork`, and `workFromFile` for an imported Markdown or text file. |
| `src/book.ts` | The book of your life: `DOMAINS` (the chapters, the Ventures of a Lifetime domains in chart order), `BELIEF_KINDS` and `checkKind` (after Aristotle, an ideal is what is best as one would pray for it, wished for as an end; a practical belief is what is best for me now, which must name the ideal it serves and the circumstances it is for), `bookEntry` (held now, or once held and no longer, with dates, kind, origins and what it serves), `compileBook` (every chapter kept; held beliefs before revisions; ideals, then practical beliefs, then unmarked; unplaced beliefs apart) and `bookMarkdown` (a dated edition grouped the same way, with citations, origins, sources and struck-through revisions). |
| `src/origins.ts` | Origins: Aristotle's seven causes of action (*Rhetoric* I.10) in his grouping, three not from the person (chance, nature, compulsion) and four from the person (habit, reasoning, spirit, appetite; origins kept as anger read as spirit); `cleanOrigins` keeps known ones once each, in his order. |
| `src/goodreads.ts` | Reading a Goodreads library export (CSV): `csvRows` (RFC 4180, quoted commas, doubled quotes, newlines) and `readGoodreads` (each row a book: title, authors, ISBN, Exclusive Shelf as want, reading or read, Date Read as the finish day). No network. |
| `src/chat.ts` | `chatJson`: one JSON reply from gpt-oss, through Vercel AI Gateway (the web app adds the OIDC token) or Ollama. Used to read a transcript and by the CLI. |
| `src/bundle.ts` | A bundle: one person's journal as one JSON document, the same from either host. Moving hosts is an export from one and an import into the other's empty journal. |
| `src/theme.ts` | The app's themes. |

Modules import each other by their `.ts` names and import JSON with `with { type: "json" }`, so Node runs them directly (`node -e 'import("./src/journal.ts")'`, Node 22.18 or later).

## Tests

`pnpm test` in `web/` runs `src/*.test.ts` with vitest. The app still calls the Postgres store's functions directly.
